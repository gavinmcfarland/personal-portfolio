Tokens are a contract

A token is not a variable. It is a promise made in two codebases at once, and the cost of breaking it is always paid by whoever is downstream of you.

Gavin McFarland 2025-11 revised 2026-03

The first design system I worked on shipped a file called colors.json and considered the job done. It was not a contract. It was a suggestion that happened to be machine-readable, and within two quarters three teams had each forked it, because a suggestion is the kind of thing you edit locally when it is inconvenient.

What makes a token a contract is not its format. It is that two parties have agreed what the name means and neither may change it alone.1 Everything else — the pipeline, the transforms, the docs — is machinery for keeping that agreement honest.

What breaks

The failure is never the rename itself. It is that a rename is cheap in the design file and expensive three repositories away, and nothing in the tooling makes that asymmetry visible at the moment of the decision. Somebody drags a swatch, and a fortnight later a native team is reading a changelog trying to work out which of forty renamed values was the one their bottom sheet used.

A token you can rename without asking anyone is not a token. It is a variable with ambitions. — the version of this I say in code review

The fix is unglamorous and mostly social: a deprecation path, a window, and a build that fails rather than warns.2 The old name keeps resolving, the pipeline emits a deprecation for it, and after the window it stops. Nobody is surprised, because the surprise was budgeted.

What that costs

It costs the ability to tidy up whenever you feel like it, which is a real loss and worth naming. A system with a deprecation policy accumulates a tail of names it does not love. The alternative is a system that cannot remove anything at all, because every removal is an incident — and that system grows until it is ignored.

So: two codebases, one promise, and a window in which breaking it is allowed as long as it is announced. That is the whole of it. The JSON is an implementation detail.

Notes

  1. 1Which is why a token pipeline owned by one team is a pipeline with one party to the contract. back
  2. 2Failing rather than warning is the entire mechanism. A warning is a deprecation nobody has to action. back
