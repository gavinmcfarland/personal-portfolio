01 Name colour(7)

colour — 16 inks, — strip geometries, — marks, and no screen anywhere.

02 Description

13 · Dither made its illustrations out of screened fields: three dot grids at mismatched pitches, clumping into something that behaved like a photograph. This set is not allowed to do that, and the replacement is not a weaker version of it — it is the other half of the same press.

A control strip is the ladder of solid patches a printer runs down the trim edge of a sheet to prove the ink is laying at density. It is a picture that is entirely about its colour, which makes it the right illustration for a set whose one inheritance from 05 · Console is the colour.

There is not a dot, a hatch, a stipple or a gradient in §3 of the stylesheet. Every mark is a solid background-color and every edge is a 1px rule. It is the first illustration engine in this folder with zero background-image declarations.

03 The sixteen f · key

The Windows 95 palette, unaltered. These sixteen do not change between surfaces: stock and ink invert when you press surface, and the palette does not. A control strip that shifted with the theme would not be a control strip.

Strip 01 — key. Eight across, two down; the only place all sixteen print together.

04 Inks and their pairings

Each ink ships with the ink it may carry type in. Every pairing below clears 4.4:1; the eight marked · are the ones this set actually sets words on, and the other eight appear as fills only.

Table scrolls sideways rather than stacking.

05 Geometries

Six ways of dividing a rectangle. The geometry is in the stylesheet, the colour is in the markup — a strip's palette is a list of data-c attributes you can read in a diff, rather than something buried in a mask.

06 Scale

Because the cells are proportional rather than fixed, a small strip shows the same picture smaller, not a crop of it — which is the exact opposite of how 13's fields behaved, and the reason these read as diagrams where those read as photographs.

Strip 08 — wedge at 96px.
Strip 09 — the same wedge at 40px. Nothing is lost but height.
Strip 10 — and at 14px, where it stops being a picture and becomes a rule. That is the .bleed you have been reading past all set.

07 Marks

Four, each meant to sit half off the edge of something. A mark that is not overlapping anything is just a shape.

crop — register marks, exact by definition.
stamp — a square ring; 13's seal without the curve.
proof tab — a flat flag, two words at most.
bleed — a rule with weight, in the flow.

08 Treatments t1–t4

The same — projects, drawn four ways from one array. If two treatments ever disagreed about the content, this page would show it.

09 Type

Two faces, neither fetched: the system UI face carries prose and the system mono carries structure. Five steps, all at 400 or 700, and rank comes from bold capitals, indent, two-digit numbering and inversion instead of from size.

11pxmicro — uppercase labels, rail numerals
12pxui — mono structure, captions, meta
13pxbody — every word of prose in the set is this size
15pxlead — command lines only; the set's one step up
20pxdisplay — one line per sheet, maximum
