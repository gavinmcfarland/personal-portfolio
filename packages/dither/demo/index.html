dither

A canvas engine for 1-bit dither textures — square dots masked into ordered geometry.

Images — a picture or an SVG, redrawn as dots

The one family that does not invent its pattern. A screenshot, a photo or an SVG is resampled onto the dot grid — area-averaged, so fine detail becomes tone rather than aliasing — and each cell gets one hard decision. Drop your own file in, pick a sample, or dither a live camera feed frame by frame.

Fields — the six hand-tuned plates

Each is a base grid plus one or two coarser grids masked into repeating geometry, so it reads as something built on a grid rather than as noise.

Auto-generated — same grammar, grown from a seed

The generator only ever varies the grid pitch, mask shape and mask scale. Same seed, same field. Click any tile to reseed just that one.

Pixel patterns — a second algorithm: coarse blocks, not dots

A different generator. Instead of fine dots clumped into geometry, it fills a small tile with symmetry — mirrored ornaments, truchet weaves, woven bars — and repeats it. Click any tile to reseed just that one.

Retro patterns — four algorithms from computing history

A third family. Ordered (Bayer) dither ramps, XOR "munching squares", elementary cellular automata (Rule 90 draws a Sierpinski triangle), and 2×3 teletext block mosaics. Click any tile to reseed it.

Retro icons — shaded 3D shapes, ordered (Bayer) dither

A silhouette — star, moon, heart, circle, hexagon, square, triangle, diamond, cross and more — rendered as a shaded 3D solid (depth, material, light, gloss) and Bayer-dithered. Click a tile to reseed it, ☆ to bookmark it.

Dithered icons — the same 3D materials, square-dot halftone

The same shaded materials, but rendered with the package's own dither dots: shadow reads as sparse quarter-dots, highlight as a solid checker. Chunkier and more printed. Click a tile to reseed it, ☆ to bookmark it.

Compositions · organic — repeated primitives, shape-following detail

Compositions · dither — the same, as 1-bit tonal gradients

The same arrangements, but each primitive is a square-dot halftone whose density is its tone — so a nest of arcs reads as a gradient the way a colour ramp does. Click a tile to reseed it, ☆ to bookmark it.
